# First ordinary local-world boat acceptance

Requires separate explicit deployment/game-testing authorization. Offline deliverables do not grant permission. Use an ordinary local test world with recoverable copies and no valuable passengers/items. Do not change any existing backup lock or allowlist.

1. For the passenger-admission candidate, record each rider’s actual AABB, native expected AABB, pose, native mount position and geometry completeness. Do not infer player geometry from rendered sitting or change the supplied water surface to pass admission. Confirm one input/queue owner, correct candidate JAR hash, Minecraft 1.21.1/NeoForge version, action session and a fresh player/world/boat observation. Record vehicle UUID/entity ID, driver, passengers, body yaw, speed, AABB and water status. A second-seat rider must be rejected before input.
2. On wide, deep still water, already manually mounted: test one straight 8–16-block leg. Verify body turning, ordinary input delivery, route progress, and no player-look-only substitute.
3. Submit 3–5 straight waypoints in one action. Preserve per-tick evidence around each waypoint and show no mandatory release/resubmit/neutral gap. Repeat a gentle turn and then a 90-degree route. Do not infer live success from formula-model tests.
4. Normal arrival must remain aboard on safe water. Verify endpoint distance, at least four slow/low-angular-speed samples, `boat_settled:true`, `input_released:true`, and combined `input_release_confirmed:true`. Record overshoot, yaw oscillation, and server correction if any.
5. While moving on a wide safe lane, STOP the exact action ID. Show immediate owned input release once processed, no further braking/turning command, and truthful `boat_settled:false` while still coasting. Verify same-ID duplicate/cancel remains terminal and a modified same-ID timeout/path fails without renewal.
6. Test fixed deadline, GUI opening, pause/unpause across deadline, direct API takeover, vehicle/player/world changes, and passenger order/count change. Do not expect hard real-time release during a frozen JVM; inspect release after execution resumes. Confirm no key or boat-input bleed into a newer owner.
7. Test rejection before unsafe travel toward unknown/unloaded edge, shallow strip, rock shore, ice, lily pads/plants, bridge/overhead block, underwater/flowing-water entry, bubble column and drop/waterfall. Put tests far enough from hazards that the retained coasting envelope remains safe. Safety refusal must never become automatic walking/combat/dismount.
8. Introduce a passive moving bystander into the forward and sideways inertia corridors; verify protection without contact. Separately observe a hostile/projectile well outside contact distance; verify stop/release, not an attack or dismount. Repeat a changing block after route admission to verify fresh safety checks.
9. Lost-response test: interrupt only the observer's read/transport, then query the original ID/session; do not resubmit with a new ID. Restarted process or world/session mismatch must not replay. A 65-point, >256-block, >16-block segment, invalid number, unknown field, append/renew field or >120-second request must reject.
10. Measure local tick cost, admission latency, realized speed, stalls/false rejections, loaded chunk boundary behavior and multiplayer/server-correction sensitivity. A single success does not accept arbitrary ocean navigation. Dynamic append/mount/dismount remain unimplemented regardless of this checklist.
